Fixed Effects

Author

Michael Fehl

Published

January 11, 2026

Theory

Motivation: Relaxing the Random Effects Assumption

The RE estimator is BLUE under its assumptions, but it requires \(\mathbb{E}[f_i \mid X_i] = 0\); the unobserved individual effect must be uncorrelated with the regressors. In many settings this fails. For example, consider a wage regression where \(f_i\) captures innate ability: individuals with higher ability self-select into more schooling, so \(f_i\) and \(x_{it}\) are correlated and RE is inconsistent.

We now consider the case where

\[ E(f_i \mid X_i) \equiv g(X_i) \neq 0 \]

for some non-constant function \(g\). This is the fixed effects assumption: \(f_i\) may be an arbitrary function of \(X_i\), so \(\text{corr}(\mathbf{x}_{it}, f_i) \neq 0\) in general. The FE estimator handles this by eliminating \(f_i\) entirely through a within-group transformation, making the correlation structure between \(f_i\) and \(X_i\) irrelevant.

Note that this comes at a cost: any regressor that is time-invariant (e.g., gender, country of birth) is absorbed by the transformation and cannot be identified under FE.

Assumptions

1. Linearity

Same as before, however in our model we now include \(\mathbf{z}_i\) to consider what happens to time-invariant regressors.

\[ y_{it} = \mathbf{x}_{it}'\beta + \mathbf{z}_i'\gamma + f_i + \mu_{it} \]

2. Fixed Individual Effects

NEW. We relax the RE assumption and allow the unobserved effect to be a non-constant function of the regressors:

\[ E(f_i \mid X_i) = g(X_i) \neq 0 \]

The implication is that \(\text{corr}(\mathbf{x}_{it}, f_i) \neq 0\) in general. Unlike random effects, \(f_i\) is treated as a fixed parameter that need not be modelled.

3. Strictly Exogenous Regressors

Same as before:

\[ \mathbb{E}[\mu_{it} \mid \mathbf{x}_{i1}, \ldots, \mathbf{x}_{iT}, f_i] \equiv \mathbb{E}[\mu_{it} \mid X_i, f_i] = 0 \]

4. Homoskedasticity

We retain homoskedasticity for the idiosyncratic error only, since \(f_i\) is no longer modelled as a random variable:

\[ \begin{cases} 1. \ \mathbb{V}\text{ar}(\mu_{it} \mid X_i, f_i) = \sigma_{\mu}^2 & \text{Constant Variance} \\ 2. \ \mathbb{C}\text{ov}(\mu_{it}, \mu_{is} \mid X_i, f_i) = 0 \quad \forall_{t \neq s} & \text{No Serial Correlation} \end{cases} \]

5. Linearly Independent Time-Variant Regressors

NEW. The rank condition is imposed on the demeaned (within-transformed) regressors \(\tilde{X}_i = M_0 X_i\), rather than on the raw regressors:

\[ \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \tilde{X}_i'\tilde{X}_i \xrightarrow{p} \underbrace{\mathbb{E}(\tilde{X}_i'\tilde{X}_i)}_{M_{\tilde{X}\tilde{X}}: \text{ (K x K)}} \text{ has rank K} \]

Since the demeaning annihilates any time-constant column, only time-varying regressors can appear in \(X_i\); any time-invariant covariates must be dropped from the model.

Implications: The Within Transformation

Since \(f_i\) is time-invariant, subtracting individual-specific means from both sides of the model eliminates it. Define:

\[ \bar{y}_{i} = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T y_{it}, \qquad \bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i} = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T \mathbf{x}_{it}, \qquad \bar{\mu}_{i} = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T \mu_{it} \]

The within (demeaned) model is:

\[ \underbrace{y_{it} - \bar{y}_{i}}_{\tilde{y}_{it}} = \underbrace{(\mathbf{x}_{it} - \bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i})'}_{\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{it}'}\beta + \underbrace{(\mu_{it} - \bar{\mu}_{i})}_{\tilde{\mu}_{it}} \]

since \(f_i - f_i = 0\). The fixed effect drops out entirely, since it is time-invariant: \(\frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^T f_i = f_i\); the same holds for \(\mathbf{z}_i\).

Projection and Annihilator Matrices

In matrix notation, define the \((T \times T)\) projection (averaging) matrix, where \(\ell_T = (1, 1, \ldots, 1)' \in \mathbb{R}^T\):

\[ P_0 = \frac{1}{T}\ell_T\ell_T' \equiv 1_T(1_T'1_T)^{-1}1_T' \]

\[ P_0 = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{1}{T} & \frac{1}{T} & \cdots \\ \frac{1}{T} & \ddots & \vdots \\ \vdots & \cdots & \frac{1}{T} \end{bmatrix} \]

Note

The projection matrix has a nice property such that \(P_Ty_t = \bar{y}_t\). For a comprehensive list of the projection matrix & annihilator matrix’s properties with subsequent proofs, see Appendix(1)

and the \((T \times T)\) annihilator (demeaning) matrix:

\[ M_0 = (I_T - P_0) \]

These satisfy \(M_0 + P_0 = I_T\), and both are symmetric and idempotent. The key property is \(M_0\ell_T = 0\): the annihilator kills any time-constant vector. The explicit form of \(M_0\) is:

\[ M_0 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 - \frac{1}{T} & -\frac{1}{T} & \cdots & -\frac{1}{T} \\ -\frac{1}{T} & 1 - \frac{1}{T} & \cdots & -\frac{1}{T} \\ \vdots & & \ddots & \vdots \\ -\frac{1}{T} & \cdots & & 1 - \frac{1}{T} \end{bmatrix} \]

For the full panel, define the \((NT \times NT)\) within-group and between-group matrices:

\[ Q = I_N \otimes M_0 \qquad F = I_N \otimes P_0 \]

satisfying \(Q + F = I_{NT}\). Since \(M_0 \ell_T = 0\), applying \(Q\) to the stacked model \(\mathbf{y}_i = X_i\beta + f_i\ell_T + \mu_i\) gives:

\[ Q\mathbf{y} = QX\beta + Q\mu \]

with \(f_i\) eliminated. The FE estimator is then OLS applied to this within-transformed system:

\[ \hat{\beta}_{FE} = (X'QX)^{-1}X'Q\mathbf{y} = \left(\sum_{i=1}^N \tilde{X}_i'\tilde{X}_i\right)^{-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^N \tilde{X}_i'\tilde{\mathbf{y}}_i\right) \]

where the last equality follows from \(Q\) being symmetric and idempotent (\(Q^2 = Q\)), so \(X'QX = \tilde{X}'\tilde{X}\) and \(X'Q\mathbf{y} = \tilde{X}'\tilde{\mathbf{y}}\).

Properties of the FE Estimator

Unbiasedness

The sampling error of \(\hat{\beta}_{FE}\) is:

\[ \begin{aligned} \hat{\beta}_{FE} - \beta &= (X'QX)^{-1}X'Q\mathbf{y} - \beta \\ &= (X'QX)^{-1}X'Q(X\beta + \mu) - \beta \\ &= (X'QX)^{-1}X'Q\mu \end{aligned} \]

Taking conditional expectations:

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}[\hat{\beta}_{FE} - \beta \mid X, f] &= (X'QX)^{-1}X'Q \underbrace{\mathbb{E}[\mu \mid X, f]}_{\text{=0 via A3}} = 0 \\ \Rightarrow \mathbb{E}[\hat{\beta}_{FE}] &= \beta \qquad \blacksquare \end{aligned} \]

Consistency

\[ \begin{aligned} \hat{\beta}_{FE} - \beta &= \underbrace{\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_i \tilde{X}_i'\tilde{X}_i\right)^{-1}}_{(1)} \underbrace{\frac{1}{N}\sum_i \tilde{X}_i'\tilde{\mu}_i}_{(2)} \\ (1) &\xrightarrow{p} M_{\tilde{X}\tilde{X}}^{-1} \text{ via LLN and CMT} \\ (2) &\xrightarrow{p} \mathbb{E}[\tilde{X}_i'\tilde{\mu}_i] = \mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{X}_i' \mathbb{E}[\tilde{\mu}_i \mid X_i, f_i]\right] = 0 \text{ via LLN and A3} \\ \hat{\beta}_{FE} &\xrightarrow{p} \beta \qquad \blacksquare \end{aligned} \]

Asymptotic Normality

\[ \begin{aligned} \sqrt{N}(\hat{\beta}_{FE} - \beta) &= \underbrace{\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_i \tilde{X}_i'\tilde{X}_i\right)^{-1}}_{(1)} \underbrace{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_i \tilde{X}_i'\tilde{\mu}_i}_{(2)} \\ (1) &\xrightarrow{p} M_{\tilde{X}\tilde{X}}^{-1} \text{ via LLN and CMT} \\ (2) &\xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}\left(0, \mathbb{V}\text{ar}(\tilde{X}_i'\tilde{\mu}_i)\right) \text{ via CLT} \end{aligned} \]

Under assumption 4, \(\mathbb{E}[\mu_i\mu_i' \mid X_i, f_i] = \sigma_\mu^2 I_T\), so:

\[ \mathbb{V}\text{ar}(\tilde{X}_i'\tilde{\mu}_i) = \mathbb{E}[\tilde{X}_i'\tilde{\mu}_i\tilde{\mu}_i'\tilde{X}_i] = \mathbb{E}[\tilde{X}_i'(\sigma_\mu^2 I_T)\tilde{X}_i] = \sigma_\mu^2 \mathbb{E}[\tilde{X}_i'\tilde{X}_i] = \sigma_\mu^2 M_{\tilde{X}\tilde{X}} \]

Thus, via Slutsky’s theorem:

\[ \sqrt{N}(\hat{\beta}_{FE} - \beta) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}\left(0, \sigma_\mu^2 M_{\tilde{X}\tilde{X}}^{-1}\right) \qquad \blacksquare \]

Estimating AVAR

The asymptotic variance under assumption 4 is \(\sigma_\mu^2 M_{\tilde{X}\tilde{X}}^{-1}\). To estimate \(\sigma_\mu^2\), use the within residuals \(\hat{\tilde{\mu}}_{it} = \tilde{y}_{it} - \tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{it}'\hat{\beta}_{FE}\) (since we showed already that \(\hat{\beta}_{FE}\) is consistent, unbiased, and asymp. normal).

Note that demeaning costs \(N\) degrees of freedom (one per individual mean subtracted), and estimating \(\beta\) costs \(K\) more; the consistent estimator of \(\sigma_\mu^2\) is:

\[ \hat{\sigma}_\mu^2 = \frac{1}{N(T-1) - K}\sum_{i=1}^N\sum_{t=1}^T \hat{\tilde{\mu}}_{it}^2 \]

Using the analogy principle:

\[ \hat{\sigma}_\mu^2 \left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \tilde{X}_i'\tilde{X}_i\right)^{-1} \xrightarrow{p} \sigma_\mu^2 \mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{X}_i'\tilde{X}_i\right]^{-1} \]

Note

For a more rigorous proof demonstrating the loss of one time period per individual and how this affects our variance estimation, see Question 2 from Problem Set 1 here

Relaxing Homoskedasticity

If assumption 4 fails (heteroskedasticity or serial correlation in \(\mu_{it}\)), the homoskedastic AVAR formula is incorrect. We instead use the cluster-robust sandwich estimator, clustering at the individual level:

\[ \widehat{\text{A}\mathbb{V}\text{ar}}(\hat{\beta}_{FE}) = \left(\sum_i \tilde{X}_i'\tilde{X}_i\right)^{-1}\left(\sum_i \tilde{X}_i'\hat{\tilde{\mu}}_i\hat{\tilde{\mu}}_i'\tilde{X}_i\right)\left(\sum_i \tilde{X}_i'\tilde{X}_i\right)^{-1} \]

This allows for arbitrary heteroskedasticity and serial correlation in \(\mu_{it}\) within each individual \(i\), placing no additional restrictions beyond assumptions 1 to 3.

xtreg y x1 x2 x3 i.year, fe vce(cluster id)

* useful when you want to include additional categorical fixed effects easily
areg y x1 x2 x3, absorb(id) vce(cluster id)

Appendix

1 Projection & Annihilator Matrix Properties

Note that the general projection matrix is \(P_X = X(X'X)^{-1}X'\), projecting onto the column space of whatever \(X\) you pass in. Here, we are projecting specifically onto the space spanned by a constant, so \(X = \ell_T\) (the \(T \times 1\) vector of ones). The properties thereby slightly shift their interpretation as a result.

1.1 Projection Matrix

1.1.1 Symmetric & Idempotent

Symmetric (\(P_0' = P_0\)):

\[ P_0' = \left(\frac{1}{T}\ell_T\ell_T'\right)' = \frac{1}{T}(\ell_T')'\ell_T' = \frac{1}{T}\ell_T\ell_T' = P_0 \qquad \blacksquare \]

Idempotent (\(P_0^2 = P_0\)):

\[ P_0^2 = \left(\frac{1}{T}\ell_T\ell_T'\right)\left(\frac{1}{T}\ell_T\ell_T'\right) = \frac{1}{T^2}\ell_T\underbrace{(\ell_T'\ell_T)}_{=T}\ell_T' = \frac{1}{T}\ell_T\ell_T' = P_0 \qquad \blacksquare \]

1.1.2 \(P_0\ell_T = \ell_T\)

\[ P_0\ell_T = \frac{1}{T}\ell_T\ell_T'\ell_T = \frac{1}{T}\ell_T \cdot T = \ell_T \qquad \blacksquare \]

More generally, \(P_0 y_i = \bar{y}_i \ell_T\). Applying the projection yields a \(T \times 1\) vector filled with the individual time mean:

\[ P_0 y_i = \frac{1}{T}\ell_T\ell_T'y_i = \frac{1}{T}\ell_T\sum_{t=1}^T y_{it} = \bar{y}_i\ell_T \qquad \blacksquare \]

1.2 Annihilator Matrix

1.2.1 Symmetric & Idempotent

Symmetric (\(M_0' = M_0\)):

\[ M_0' = (I_T - P_0)' = I_T' - P_0' = I_T - P_0 = M_0 \qquad \blacksquare \]

Idempotent (\(M_0^2 = M_0\)):

\[ M_0^2 = (I_T - P_0)^2 = I_T - 2P_0 + P_0^2 = I_T - 2P_0 + P_0 = I_T - P_0 = M_0 \qquad \blacksquare \]

1.2.2 \(M_0\ell_T = 0\)

\[ M_0\ell_T = (I_T - P_0)\ell_T = \ell_T - P_0\ell_T = \ell_T - \ell_T = 0 \qquad \blacksquare \]

More generally, \(M_0 y_i = \tilde{y}_i\). Applying the annihilator yields the demeaned vector:

\[ M_0 y_i = (I_T - P_0)y_i = y_i - \bar{y}_i\ell_T = \tilde{y}_i \qquad \blacksquare \]

And since \(\mu_i\) is just another \(T \times 1\) vector, the same argument gives \(\tilde{\mu}_i = M_0\mu_i\); that is, the demeaned error is exactly the annihilator applied to the true idiosyncratic error.

1.2.3 \(M_0 P_0 = 0\) (Orthogonality)

\(P_0\) and \(M_0\) are orthogonal complements: the “mean part” and the “demeaned part” of any vector are orthogonal.

\[ M_0 P_0 = (I_T - P_0)P_0 = P_0 - P_0^2 = P_0 - P_0 = 0 \qquad \blacksquare \]
