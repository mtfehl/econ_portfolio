Regression Discontinuity Designs

Author

Michael Fehl

Published

February 21, 2026

Theory

Introduction

Natural or quasi-experiments try to mimic the situation of randomized controlled trials by exploiting variation that is arguably as good as randomly assigned. A special case arises when assignment to treatment is based on a cutoff in an observable variable.

The key ingredients of a regression discontinuity design (RDD) are: at least one observable covariate (the “running” or “assignment” variable) and at least one cutoff that determines assignment to treatment. The key intuition is that units just below and just above the cutoff should be similar in all respects except treatment status. Hence, differences in outcomes at the cutoff can be attributed to the treatment.

RD designs come in two varieties:

  • Sharp RD (SRD): treatment status is a deterministic function of the assignment variable at the cutoff. \(D_i = \mathbf{1}[X_i \geq c]\).
  • Fuzzy RD (FRD): treatment status is a discontinuous stochastic function of the assignment variable at the cutoff. Crossing the cutoff makes it more likely that the unit is treated, but the jump in \(P(D_i = 1)\) is not from 0 to 1. This is analogous to imperfect compliance in an RCT, with cutoff crossing \(Z_i = \mathbf{1}[X_i \geq c]\) serving as the instrument for \(D_i\).

Identification in the Sharp RDD

Define the following notation:

  • \(Y_i\): outcome
  • \(X_i\): assignment (running) variable
  • \(D_i = \mathbf{1}[X_i \geq c]\): binary treatment status
  • \(\beta_i\): individual treatment effect
  • \(c\): cutoff
  • \(U_i\): unobservables, other factors that affect \(Y_i\)

We make two assumptions:

A1. Homogeneous treatment effect: \(\beta_i = \beta_D\)

A2. Continuity: \(\mathbb{E}[U_i \mid X_i = x]\) is continuous in \(x\)

Under A1 and A2, the RD gap identifies \(\beta_D\). To see this, write the linear regression model \(Y_i = \beta_0 + \beta_D D_i + \delta X_i + U_i\). Then:

\[ \begin{aligned} &\lim_{x \downarrow c} \mathbb{E}[Y_i \mid X_i = x] - \lim_{x \uparrow c} \mathbb{E}[Y_i \mid X_i = x] \\ &= \lim_{x \downarrow c} \mathbb{E}(\beta_0 + \beta_D D_i + \delta X_i + U_i \mid X_i = x) - \lim_{x \uparrow c} \mathbb{E}(\beta_0 + \beta_D D_i + \delta X_i + U_i \mid X_i = x) \\ &= \beta_D + \delta c + \mathbb{E}(U_i \mid X_i = c) - \beta_D \cdot 0 - \delta c - \lim_{x \uparrow c}\mathbb{E}(U_i \mid X_i = x) \\ &= \beta_D \end{aligned} \]

where the last equality uses A2. Without A1 (heterogeneous effects), the RD gap identifies the conditional average treatment effect at the cutoff:

\[ \lim_{x \downarrow c} \mathbb{E}[Y_i \mid X_i = x] - \lim_{x \uparrow c} \mathbb{E}[Y_i \mid X_i = x] = \mathbb{E}(\beta_i \mid X_i = c) \]

Note that under A2, \(\mathbb{E}(U_i \mid X_i = c, D_i = 0) = \mathbb{E}(U_i \mid X_i = c, D_i = 1)\), so \(U_i\) is mean-independent of \(D_i\) at \(c\). This means we can test A2 indirectly by checking whether observed covariates \(W_i\) satisfy \(\mathbb{E}(W_i \mid X_i = x)\) continuous at \(c\) – analogous to balance checks in RCTs.

Imprecise Control (Lee-Lemieux)

A2 is implied by an alternative assumption A2’ (imprecise control): \(P(X_i = x \mid U_i = u)\) is continuous in \(x\) for all \(u\). This states that the density of \(X_i\) must be smooth for all “types” of agents. It allows for different agents to have different levels of control over \(X_i\), as long as none of them can control \(X_i\) completely or make sure it is always above the cutoff. Under A2’, A2 follows from Bayes’ rule:

\[ \mathbb{E}(U_i \mid X_i = x) = \sum_u \frac{P(X_i = x \mid U_i = u)P(U_i = u)}{\sum_u P(X_i = x \mid U_i = u) P(U_i = u)} u \]

A practical implication of A2’ is that the density of the running variable should be smooth at the cutoff. A manipulation test (McCrary density test) checks for a discontinuity in the density of \(X_i\) at \(c\).

The Local Nature of the RDD Estimate

RDD estimates are local: they identify treatment effects only for units with \(X_i \approx c\). In the absence of additional assumptions, they are not informative about treatment effects at other values of the score. This is a fundamental external validity limitation of the design.

Identification in the Fuzzy RDD

Homogeneous Treatment Effects

When treatment status is influenced by factors beyond \(X_i\), so \(D_i \neq \mathbf{1}[X_i \geq c]\), there must be a first stage: a discontinuity in the likelihood of treatment at the cutoff.

A3. First stage: \(\lim_{x \downarrow c} P(D_i = 1 \mid X_i = x) - \lim_{x \uparrow c} P(D_i = 1 \mid X_i = x) > 0\)

A4. Exclusion restriction: \(\beta_Z = 0\) (crossing the cutoff affects outcomes only through treatment, not directly)

The data generating process is:

\[ Y_i = \beta_0 + \beta_D D_i + \delta X_i + \beta_Z Z_i + U_i \qquad D_i = \pi_0 + \pi_Z Z_i + \pi_X X_i + V_i \]

Under A1-A4, the ratio of RD gaps in \(Y_i\) and \(D_i\) at the cutoff identifies \(\beta_D\):

\[ \frac{\lim_{x \downarrow c} \mathbb{E}[Y_i \mid X_i = x] - \lim_{x \uparrow c} \mathbb{E}[Y_i \mid X_i = x]}{\lim_{x \downarrow c} \mathbb{E}[D_i \mid X_i = x] - \lim_{x \uparrow c} \mathbb{E}[D_i \mid X_i = x]} = \beta_D \]

Proof: The numerator (reduced form / ITT at the cutoff):

\[ \begin{aligned} &\lim_{x \downarrow c} \mathbb{E}[Y_i \mid X_i = x] - \lim_{x \uparrow c} \mathbb{E}[Y_i \mid X_i = x] \\ &= \beta_D [E(D_i \mid X_i = c^+) - E(D_i \mid X_i = c^-)] + \beta_Z \quad \text{under A1, A2} \\ &= \beta_D \pi_Z + \beta_Z \\ &= \beta_D \pi_Z \quad \text{under A4} \end{aligned} \]

Dividing by the first stage \(\pi_Z\) gives \(\beta_D\). \(\blacksquare\)

The RD gap in \(Y_i\) alone (the reduced form) is also referred to as the intention-to-treat effect at the cutoff. It does not depend on the validity of the exclusion restriction and is sometimes policy-relevant in its own right.

Heterogeneous Treatment Effects

Once we drop A1 and allow for heterogeneous effects \(\beta_i\) and heterogeneous compliance \(\pi_i\), the data generating process is:

\[ Y_i = \beta_0 + \beta_i D_i + \delta X_i + \beta_Z Z_i + U_i \qquad D_i = \pi_0 + \pi_i Z_i + \pi_X X_i + V_i \]

Under A2-A4 with no cross-overs (one-sided non-compliance), the ratio of reduced forms identifies the ATT at the cutoff:

\[ \frac{\lim_{x \downarrow c} \mathbb{E}[Y_i \mid X_i = x] - \lim_{x \uparrow c} \mathbb{E}[Y_i \mid X_i = x]}{\lim_{x \downarrow c} \mathbb{E}[D_i \mid X_i = x] - \lim_{x \uparrow c} \mathbb{E}[D_i \mid X_i = x]} = \mathbb{E}(\beta_i \mid D_i = 1, X_i = c) \]

Under A2-A4 with two-sided non-compliance, the ratio identifies a weighted average treatment effect at the cutoff:

\[ \frac{\lim_{x \downarrow c} \mathbb{E}[Y_i \mid X_i = x] - \lim_{x \uparrow c} \mathbb{E}[Y_i \mid X_i = x]}{\lim_{x \downarrow c} \mathbb{E}[D_i \mid X_i = x] - \lim_{x \uparrow c} \mathbb{E}[D_i \mid X_i = x]} = \mathbb{E}\left(\beta_i \frac{\pi_i}{\mathbb{E}(\pi_i)} \,\Big|\, X_i = c\right) \]

Under A2-A5 (monotonicity: \(P(\pi_i = -1 \mid X_i = c) = 0\), ruling out defiers at the cutoff), the ratio identifies the local average treatment effect (LATE) for compliers at the cutoff:

\[ \frac{\lim_{x \downarrow c} \mathbb{E}[Y_i \mid X_i = x] - \lim_{x \uparrow c} \mathbb{E}[Y_i \mid X_i = x]}{\lim_{x \downarrow c} \mathbb{E}[D_i \mid X_i = x] - \lim_{x \uparrow c} \mathbb{E}[D_i \mid X_i = x]} = \mathbb{E}(\beta_i \mid X_i = c, \pi_i = 1) \]

The proportion of compliers at the cutoff is given by the first stage: \(P(\pi_i = 1 \mid X_i = c) = \lim_{x \downarrow c} \mathbb{E}[D_i \mid X_i = x] - \lim_{x \uparrow c} \mathbb{E}[D_i \mid X_i = x]\).

Estimation in the Sharp RDD

The RD estimand is the gap in \(Y_i\) at the cutoff:

\[ \hat{\tau}_{SRD} = \lim_{x \downarrow c} \mathbb{E}[Y_i \mid X_i = x] - \lim_{x \uparrow c} \mathbb{E}[Y_i \mid X_i = x] = \mu_+ - \mu_- \]

In practice there are often few observations right around the cutoff. A natural solution is to include observations further away and fit a local polynomial on each side. The pooled specification within a \(\pm h\) bandwidth is:

\[ Y_i = \mu_- + (\mu_+ - \mu_-)\mathbf{1}[X_i \geq c] + \mu_{-,1}(X_i - c) + (\mu_{+,1} - \mu_{-,1})(X_i - c)\mathbf{1}[X_i \geq c] + U_i' \]

The coefficient on \(\mathbf{1}[X_i \geq c]\) is the RD point estimate \(\hat{\mu}_+ - \hat{\mu}_-\).

Local Polynomial Methods

While the true regression function might be approximately linear close to the cutoff, a larger bandwidth \(h\) makes it more likely that the function exhibits curvature. Local polynomial methods implement polynomial regression fits using only observations near the cutoff, within a bandwidth \(h\):

For observations with \(X_i \geq c\):

\[ Y_i = \mu_+ + \mu_{+,1}(X_i - c) + \mu_{+,2}(X_i - c)^2 + \ldots + \mu_{+,p}(X_i - c)^p + \varepsilon_i \]

using \(K\!\left(\frac{X_i - c}{h}\right)\) as a weight for each observation. Symmetrically for \(X_i < c\). The intercept estimates \(\hat{\mu}_+\) and \(\hat{\mu}_-\) are estimates of \(\lim_{x \downarrow c} \mathbb{E}[Y_i \mid X_i = x]\) and \(\lim_{x \uparrow c} \mathbb{E}[Y_i \mid X_i = x]\).

The three key choices are:

  1. Kernel function \(K(\cdot)\): assigns non-negative weights based on distance to \(c\). The recommended choice is the triangular kernel \(K(u) = (1-|u|)\mathbf{1}(|u| \leq 1)\), which assigns maximum weight at \(X_i = c\) and declines linearly. In practice results are not sensitive to the kernel choice.

  2. Bandwidth \(h\): controls the width of the neighborhood. Smaller \(h\) reduces misspecification bias but increases variance (fewer observations). There is a fundamental bias-variance trade-off. The optimal bandwidth minimizes the asymptotic MSE:

\[ AMSE_{\hat{\tau}_p}(h) = \underbrace{h^{2(p+1)} B_p^2}_{\text{Bias}^2} + \underbrace{\frac{1}{nh} V_p}_{\text{Variance}} \]

The optimal bandwidth is:

\[ h_{opt} = \left(\frac{V_p}{2(p+1)B_p^2}\right)^{1/(2p+3)} \cdot n^{-1/(2p+3)} \]

The Calonico, Cattaneo and Titiunik (2014) procedure estimates \(h_{opt}\) by estimating the unknown bias \(B_p\) using a local polynomial of order \(q = p+1\) with a pilot bandwidth \(b\).

  1. Polynomial order \(p\): higher order reduces bias for a given bandwidth but increases variance. The AMSE formula can be used to select the optimal order.

Robust Bias Correction Inference

Since the optimal bandwidth is chosen to minimize AMSE, there is a residual bias term \(\mathcal{B}\) that should be accounted for in inference. The robust bias-corrected confidence interval is:

\[ \text{CI}_{rbc} = \left[(\hat{\tau} - \hat{\mathcal{B}}) \pm 1.96 \cdot \sqrt{\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{bc}}\right] \]

where \(\hat{\mathcal{B}}\) is the estimated bias and \(\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{bc}\) is a bias-corrected variance estimator (larger than the conventional standard error to account for the variability of \(\hat{\mathcal{B}}\)).

Estimation in the Fuzzy RDD

In fuzzy RDD, we additionally need to estimate the RD gap in \(D_i\):

\[ \lim_{x \downarrow c} \mathbb{E}[D_i \mid X_i = x] - \lim_{x \uparrow c} \mathbb{E}[D_i \mid X_i = x] = \alpha_+ - \alpha_- \]

The pooled local linear specification for the first stage is:

\[ D_i = \alpha_- + (\alpha_+ - \alpha_-)\mathbf{1}[X_i \geq c] + \alpha_{-,1}(X_i - c) + (\alpha_{+,1} - \alpha_{-,1})(X_i - c)\mathbf{1}[X_i \geq c] + V_i \]

The fuzzy RD point estimate is the ratio:

\[ \hat{\tau}_{FRD} = \frac{\hat{\mu}_+ - \hat{\mu}_-}{\hat{\alpha}_+ - \hat{\alpha}_-} \]

In practice, the LATE estimate is obtained by using the IV estimator, instrumenting \(D_i\) with \(Z_i = \mathbf{1}[X_i \geq c]\) and including \((X_i - c)\) and \((X_i - c)\mathbf{1}[X_i \geq c]\) as regressors within the bandwidth.

The recommended procedure is to use robust local polynomial methods, analogous to the sharp RD setting. Optimal bandwidth selection for the LATE typically relies on a single bandwidth for both numerator and denominator.

Applied

* --- rdrobust: main RD estimation package ---
* ssc install rdrobust

* --- sharp RDD ---
* y: outcome; x: running variable; c: cutoff (default 0; subtract c from x)
gen x_centered = x - cutoff

* local linear, triangular kernel, MSE-optimal bandwidth (CCT 2014)
rdrobust y x_centered, kernel(triangular) p(1)

* local quadratic
rdrobust y x_centered, kernel(triangular) p(2)

* specify bandwidth manually
rdrobust y x_centered, kernel(triangular) p(1) h(5)

* --- fuzzy RDD ---
* fuzzy(D): D is the treatment receipt variable
rdrobust y x_centered, fuzzy(D) kernel(triangular) p(1)

* --- rddensity: McCrary density test for manipulation ---
* H0: density of x is continuous at the cutoff (no manipulation)
* ssc install rddensity
rddensity x_centered

* --- rdplot: visualize the RD ---
* generates binned scatterplot with polynomial fits on each side
rdplot y x_centered, p(1)

* --- local linear regression manually (for transparency) ---
* restrict to bandwidth around cutoff
gen treatment = (x_centered >= 0)
gen x_treat = x_centered * treatment

* pooled local linear within bandwidth h
reg y treatment x_centered x_treat if abs(x_centered) <= h, vce(robust)

* --- covariate balance test ---
* run rdrobust on predetermined covariates; expect tau ~ 0
rdrobust w1 x_centered, kernel(triangular) p(1)
rdrobust w2 x_centered, kernel(triangular) p(1)

* --- fuzzy RDD via IV manually ---
* instrument: Z = 1[X >= c]; endogenous: D
ivreg2 y (D = treatment) x_centered x_treat if abs(x_centered) <= h, robust
